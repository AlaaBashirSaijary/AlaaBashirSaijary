From a paper notebook to a clinic program: a practical plan

Moving a working clinic to software is easier in small steps than in one weekend. This plan keeps you safe while you switch.

Last updated: 24 November 2026

1. Decide what must move

You do not need every old page on day one. Start with active patients and anything critical: allergies, chronic conditions, regular medication. Older files can stay on paper, or move gradually when those patients return.

2. Prepare a simple list

Put the basics in a spreadsheet: name, phone, file number. Clean obvious duplicates and typos. If your program can import Excel or CSV files, this one list saves you from typing every patient by hand.

3. Shape the exam form to your specialty

Write down what you record in a typical visit. A good program lets you build the exam form around those fields, so you are not forced into someone else’s template.

4. Run both side by side for two weeks

Keep the notebook and the program in parallel. This is the safest moment to find what you miss. If the program has a free trial, use it as this parallel run, before you pay anything.

5. Set up locks and backups on day one

Set the device lock, the app lock and a backup routine before real patients are in the system, not after. Our guide on protecting patient data covers the checklist.

6. Train your staff

Show the receptionist and any assistant the three things they will do every day: find a patient, book an appointment, and record a payment. Give them their own accounts.

7. Retire the notebook

When two weeks pass without you reaching for the notebook, stop writing in it. Keep it somewhere safe for old records.

What to expect

The first week feels slower, because you are learning. Most of the gain shows up when you search a patient in seconds, see the day’s appointments at a glance, and print a prescription without rewriting it.
